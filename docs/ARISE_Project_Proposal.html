Academic & Technical Project Proposal · Revision 2

ARISE: Anisotropy-Resolved Isomer Scoring Engine

Formerly ADSEE (Anisotropy-Aware De Novo Structural Elucidation Engine)

1. Executive Summary & Objective

Computer-Assisted Structure Elucidation (CASE) has two main strands today. One is a mature, mostly rule-based family of tools: commercial systems such as ACD/Structure Elucidator, Mnova Structure Elucidation and Bruker CMC-se, and open systems such as LSD/pyLSD and Sherlock. The other is a fast-growing body of machine-learning and LLM-agent work. Rule-based systems are interpretable, but they typically rank candidate structures with chemical shifts predicted from bond connectivity alone (for example HOSE codes), most often for 13C. Such predictors cannot see through-space effects. In folded small molecules with several aromatic rings, ring currents shield or deshield protons that sit above or beside a ring. This can shift 1H resonances by more than 1 ppm, enough to make the wrong regioisomer the top-ranked match. Recent ML and agentic methods report strong benchmark results, but they remain opaque and their authors report run-to-run variability.

ARISE (Anisotropy-Resolved Isomer Scoring Engine) addresses this gap. It is a lightweight, deterministic and fully explainable scoring and re-ranking engine. It decides between closely related candidate structures, chiefly regioisomers, using 1H data and a physics-based 3D anisotropy correction. Candidates come from a local compound library filtered by exact mass, or from a SMILES list produced by another tool such as Sherlock or NMRfilter. ARISE then outputs a ranked list with a per-stage score breakdown, so every decision can be traced back to experimental evidence. The target is under one second end to end on consumer hardware. ARISE is delivered as a native command-line tool and as a serverless WebAssembly page that runs offline.

Why the name changed. The original title claimed de novo elucidation, but the pipeline searches a library or re-ranks supplied candidates; it does not build new structures. The new name describes the actual contribution: resolving isomers using anisotropy. Generating structures de novo (see Section 8) is planned as a v2 extension.

2. Related Work & Positioning

A review of current open-source and commercial CASE tools shaped the scope of ARISE. The closest open system, Sherlock (Molecules, 2023), solves most of its 45 test problems in 1–10 s. However:

  • it ranks candidates on 13C shifts only;
  • it does not model 3D geometry or anisotropy, and its test set used planar structures;
  • it requires a molecular formula and HSQC, HMBC and COSY spectra;
  • it runs as a Docker server (8 GB+ RAM recommended);
  • five of its 45 test problems could not be solved within three hours because generating every candidate structure is combinatorially explosive.

NMRfilter already combines candidates retrieved by mass with predicted HSQC/HMBC matching. Physical ring-current models (Johnson–Bovey, Haigh–Mallion) are standard in protein shift predictors and in Abraham’s CHARGE program, and DFT-based methods such as DP4+ handle anisotropy from first principles at a cost of hours per molecule. ARISE therefore positions itself as complementary to these tools:

Gap in existing open CASE toolsARISE’s response
Ranking based on 13C onlyRanks primarily on 1H, where ring-current effects are largest
No 3D geometry, stereochemistry or anisotropy3D conformer ensembles with a physical ring-current and bond-anisotropy correction
Connectivity-only prediction cannot separate folded regioisomersRegioisomer separation in folded multi-aromatic molecules is the primary benchmark target
Requires a full 2D data set (HSQC + HMBC + COSY) and formulaWorks from HRMS + 1H alone; 2D data refine the ranking when available
Server/Docker deployment; data leave the clientStatic WebAssembly page plus native CLI; offline, data stay local
Exhaustive structure generation can take hoursBounded candidate sets; sub-second scoring

Because ARISE accepts external SMILES lists, it can directly re-rank the top-N output of Sherlock or NMRfilter. This makes the comparison with existing tools cooperative rather than adversarial.

3. Core Logic & Algorithmic Pipeline

ARISE prunes candidates in stages, from coarse to fine. Each stage is more precise and more expensive than the one before it, and works on a smaller pool. Every stage records its score contribution, so the final ranking can be fully explained. Candidates are ranked by total score rather than discarded outright, so one ambiguous measurement cannot eliminate the correct structure.

Stage 1: Candidate Sourcing & Exact-Mass Filtration

There are two entry points. (a) Library mode: a high-resolution monoisotopic mass (±0.0005 Da, Orbitrap/Q-TOF) is converted into candidate molecular formulas. Formulas are filtered with the Seven Golden Rules (element-ratio limits, nitrogen rule, isotope-pattern fit) and with carbon and proton counts read from the NMR data. The surviving formulas are then matched in a single linear scan against a packed local flat-file library curated from open repositories (PubChem, ChEBI, COCONUT), typically leaving about 100–500 isomers. (b) Re-ranking mode: a SMILES list from an external tool is loaded directly.

Stage 2: 1D Spin-System Checklist (Graph-Topological Filter)

Users tag clearly visible spin systems in the high-field 1H spectrum, such as a para-disubstituted ring, an isolated ethyl group or a tert-butyl singlet. A curated dictionary translates each tag into a SMARTS pattern, and RDKit substructure matching tests every candidate. Candidates whose bond graph cannot produce a tagged spin system are heavily penalized. No chemical shift simulation is needed at this stage, which typically reduces the pool to about 5–12 candidates.

Stage 3: Baseline 1H Shift Prediction

Each surviving candidate receives baseline 1H shift predictions from a HOSE-code lookup table built from the openly available nmrshiftdb2 assigned spectra. Each proton’s bonded neighborhood is encoded out to 4–6 bond spheres. When no match is found at the full depth, the lookup falls back to fewer spheres. The method is deterministic, transparent and fast, and the table ships as a packed flat file.

Stage 4: Physical Anisotropy Correction

HOSE codes describe connectivity only, so they cannot capture through-space shielding from rings far apart in the bond graph. For each candidate, ARISE generates an ensemble of 20–50 3D conformers with RDKit’s ETKDG method, refines them with the MMFF94 force field, and averages the results with Boltzmann weights. Two classical models supply the correction:

  • Aromatic ring currents: the Johnson–Bovey model (or the Haigh–Mallion model), which remains accurate close to the ring face, where the point-dipole approximation breaks down.
  • Bond anisotropy (C=O, C≡C, C=C): the McConnell point-dipole equation,

Δσ = (Δχ / 3r3) · (1 − 3cos2θ)

where Δχ is the magnetic susceptibility anisotropy of the group, r is the distance from the group’s center to the proton, and θ is the angle to the group’s symmetry axis. To avoid counting effects already present in the HOSE baseline twice, the correction is applied only to proton–group pairs more than four bonds apart. The corrected 1H shifts are compared with the experimental peak list, and the resulting error is the main scoring term for separating regioisomers.

Stage 5: 2D Correlation Scoring (The Ruler Method)

When HSQC and HMBC peak lists are provided, ARISE first assigns peaks to atoms. Each HSQC cross-peak is matched to a specific C–H pair of the candidate by weighted bipartite matching (the Hungarian algorithm), using the Stage 3–4 predicted shifts as costs. HMBC correlations are then scored against bond distances in the candidate’s graph, precomputed by breadth-first search (BFS) from each atom:

  • 2–3 bonds: no penalty;
  • 4 bonds: small penalty (these correlations are common in aromatic and W-shaped geometries);
  • 5 or more bonds, or no path: large penalty.

This is constrained peak assignment followed by graph-distance scoring, not subgraph isomorphism, and it runs in polynomial time on molecules of this size.

4. Target Minimalist Architecture

  • Reference prototype (Python + RDKit): a throwaway implementation used to test the scientific hypothesis (Phase 0) and to generate reference outputs for testing the native engine.
  • Core engine (C++20): a native engine with tight data layouts and SIMD-friendly geometry loops for the conformer and anisotropy stage, which is the only computationally heavy step.
  • Cheminformatics backend: RDKit’s C++ API, limited to the modules required (GraphMol, SmilesParse, Substruct, DistGeomHelpers, ForceFieldHelpers) to keep the WebAssembly download small.
  • Interfaces: a CLI comes first (arise --mass … --h1 peaks.csv [--hsqc …] [--hmbc …] [--features …] [--candidates list.smi]). A minimal single-page web front end later calls the same entry point.
  • Compilation target: the same codebase is compiled to WebAssembly with Emscripten for a static, serverless single-page app. The first build is single-threaded, since Stage 4 handles only 5–12 candidates. Multithreading would require cross-origin isolation headers (COOP/COEP) from the static host.
  • Data storage: packed local flat files (candidate library, HOSE 1H table, SMARTS dictionary). No database server is needed, and the tool runs in offline, air-gapped environments.

6. Phase-Gate Development Blueprint

Phase 0: Scientific Feasibility (Go/No-Go)

  • Milestone 0.1: Assemble 20–40 sets of isomers with assigned experimental 1H shifts where ring currents matter (cyclophanes, biaryls/atropisomers, folded aromatic amides, natural products with stacked rings), from nmrshiftdb2 and the supporting information of published papers.
  • Milestone 0.2: Implement the HOSE 1H baseline and the conformer-ensemble anisotropy correction in Python.
  • Milestone 0.3: Compare top-1 accuracy, mean rank of the true isomer, and 1H mean absolute error with and without the correction. Gate: proceed only if the correction gives a clear improvement on the anisotropy-heavy subset (target: at least 10–15 percentage points in top-1 accuracy).

Phase 1: Interfaces & Data Foundation

  • Milestone 1.1: Fix the input formats (1H peak list; optional HSQC/HMBC CSV; spin-system tags; SMILES or library candidates) and the ranked-output format with per-stage scores.
  • Milestone 1.2: Build the SMARTS spin-system dictionary (ortho/meta/para substitution, aromatic heterocycles, alkyl branch motifs).
  • Milestone 1.3: Build and pack the HOSE 1H table and the mass-indexed candidate library.

Phase 2: Native C++ Engine & CLI

  • Milestone 2.1: Set up the CMake build linking RDKit, and write a zero-allocation streaming CSV reader for library scans.
  • Milestone 2.2: Port Stages 1–5. Speed targets: Stages 1–2 over 500 candidates in under 50 ms; full pipeline on 12 candidates in under 1 s.
  • Milestone 2.3: Test that native scores match the Python reference within tolerance across the Phase 0 dataset.

Phase 3: WebAssembly Deployment

  • Milestone 3.1: Build RDKit (required modules only) and the engine with Emscripten.
  • Milestone 3.2: Build a minimal static page with a command-line-style interface. Verify identical rankings to the native CLI and correct operation with the network disabled.

Phase 4: Validation, Benchmarking & Manuscript

  • Milestone 4.1: Test on real historical spectral datasets, including blinded structure challenges provided by faculty.
  • Milestone 4.2: Benchmark against Sherlock, NMRfilter, the plain nmrshiftdb2 predictor and an ML predictor on the folded-aromatic regioisomer set, and run a re-ranking experiment on Sherlock’s top-N output.
  • Milestone 4.3: Draft the manuscript, positioning ARISE as a fast, physics-based middle ground between connectivity-only prediction and DFT-level methods.

7. Risks & Limitations

  • Scientific risk: classical ring-current models may not separate isomers reliably enough. Phase 0 tests this before any engineering investment.
  • Library-constrained: in library mode, ARISE cannot find compounds absent from the library. Re-ranking mode and the v2 generator (Section 8) address this.
  • Data coverage: open assigned 1H data are sparser than 13C data, so HOSE lookups will fall back to shallower spheres more often.
  • Conformational flexibility: force-field ensembles may misjudge conformer populations for flexible or strongly solvated molecules.
  • Stereochemistry: only partly addressed through 3D conformers; full stereochemical assignment is out of scope for v1.
  • Engineering: a full WebAssembly build of RDKit with force-field support is the riskiest build task, so it is deliberately scheduled after the native engine is validated.

8. Future Work (v2)

  • True de novo mode: connect an open-source structure generator (e.g., surge) constrained by Stage 2 fragments, so that ARISE scores generated structures instead of library entries.
  • 13C anisotropy terms and combined 1H/13C scoring.
  • Multithreaded WebAssembly build for larger candidate pools.
